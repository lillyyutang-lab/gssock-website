Custom Sock Manufacturing: A Practical Guide for First Orders

A well-planned first order is less about knowing every technical term and more about making the right decisions in the right sequence.

Last updated: October 30, 2026 · Written by GoodStartSocks

Custom sock manufacturing begins long before a machine starts knitting. The clearer the project direction is at the beginning, the easier it is to turn a design into a sample that can be approved with confidence. This guide is for brands, teams, retailers and promotional buyers planning their first factory-direct sock project.

GoodStartSocks is an OEM and ODM custom sock manufacturer based in Zhuji, Zhejiang, China. We support buyers through project planning, sample development, bulk production and packaging discussions. A standard order starts from 500 pairs.

1. Define the role of the socks

Start with the job the finished socks must do. A retail collection, a sports team program, a studio grip sock and a corporate gift may all use socks, but they do not start from the same priorities. Decide whether the emphasis is everyday comfort, performance, brand presentation, gifting or retail display.

This first decision informs the height, material direction, logo placement, size approach and packaging level. Browse our custom sock styles to compare possible starting points, then use the sock height guide to make the desired silhouette clear.

2. Turn visual ideas into production information

A logo file and a moodboard are useful, but they work best when paired with a few practical notes. Tell the factory where the logo should sit, how many colours are important, whether a pattern should repeat, and which details should remain visible when the sock is worn and stretched.

Useful files and references

  • Logo artwork or a clear reference image
  • Colour references and notes on priority colours
  • Preferred sock style and height
  • Examples of the desired packaging or retail presentation
  • Any elements that must not be changed during development

For knitted designs, highly detailed text and very fine lines may need to be simplified so the result is readable in the finished construction. It is better to review that trade-off during sampling than after bulk production begins.

3. Make the quantity and size plan usable

Quantity is more than a quote field. It affects the production route, colour allocation, size breakdown and packaging plan. GoodStartSocks has a standard starting MOQ of 500 pairs. The final production plan depends on the selected construction, yarns, logo technique, size range and packaging.

If the order serves more than one customer group, list the expected pair count for each size group. Even an estimated mix is more useful than leaving sizes until the end. It lets the factory discuss the practical fit and labeling plan earlier.

4. Treat the sample as a decision point

A sample should be evaluated as a product, not only as a photograph. Review it laid flat and worn. Look at the colour direction, logo clarity, comfort, cuff feel, heel position, toe area, stretch and recovery. If the project includes packaging, check whether the sample sits well in the selected presentation format too.

The usual guide for sample preparation is about 3–7 days. Changes are normal at this stage; the purpose is to identify them before the order moves into bulk production.

5. Create an approval reference for bulk production

Once the sample is approved, preserve the approved sock, the corresponding artwork version and any measurement or packaging notes together. This small reference set prevents different stakeholders from working from different versions later. It is also useful for repeat orders.

Bulk production is typically about 30 days after sample approval. The final timing will depend on the project specifications, quantity, packaging, production schedule and shipping arrangement, so target delivery dates should be shared at the start.

6. Plan packaging as part of the product

Packaging has a practical role as well as a visual one. It can protect the socks, carry size or care information, and create the intended retail or gift experience. Discuss bands, hang tags, labels, bags, boxes or gift-ready formats before the production plan is locked.

Visit our project examples to see packaging and presentation formats. These examples are shared as format inspiration only and do not imply named-brand partnerships.

7. Send one complete brief for an accurate discussion

A factory can help refine technical decisions, but it needs a clear starting point. Combine the intended sock type, artwork, estimated quantity, size range, material direction, packaging idea, destination and target date in one message. Our project-brief checklist gives you a simple format to use.
